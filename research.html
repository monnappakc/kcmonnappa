Doctoral research

A thesis on India's strategic autonomy read through Ontological Security Theory.

Thesis

India's Pursuit of Ontological Security through the Policy of Strategic Autonomy

In progress

The thesis applies Ontological Security Theory to Indian foreign policy from 1947 to 2026. It argues that strategic autonomy is more than a policy stance. It is an identity commitment that a state keeps in order to remain recognisably itself.

The work is in its final stages. Seven chapters have been drafted and revised. The field component draws on practitioner interviews, a focus group in Shillong, and a Northeast case study.

Affiliation: Department of Political Science, North-Eastern Hill University, Shillong. Supervisor: Professor T. T. Haokip.

Original concepts
Sovereignty hypersensitivity

The heightened reaction a state shows when it senses that its freedom of action is under threat. The concept explains why small adjustments in external relations can draw a disproportionate response.

Minimum viable identity

The smallest set of commitments a state must hold to stay recognisably itself while its external circumstances change.

Narrative labour

The continuing work of telling a national story so that a given foreign policy appears consistent with who the country is.
